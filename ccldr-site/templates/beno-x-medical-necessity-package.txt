=== PART 1 — THE BENO-X ARGUMENT, IN ORDER ===

1. The government created a licensing/exemption regime for medical
   cannabis access (MMAR → MMPR → ACMPR).
2. Courts have repeatedly found that regime, in whole or in part, violated
   section 7 of the Charter (life, liberty, security of the person).
3. A constitutionally defective exemption regime is not a valid exemption.
4. If no valid exemption exists for a person with a genuine medical need,
   possession outside that defective regime cannot constitute a criminal
   offense as applied to that person.
5. Therefore: Bad Exemption = No Offense.

This is a constitutional argument about the validity of the regulatory
scheme as applied to the accused's specific medical circumstances — it is
not a claim that cannabis possession is generally lawful.

=== PART 2 — THE FIVE PILLARS (CASE LAW CHRONOLOGY) ===

PILLAR 1 — R v Parker, 2000 CanLII 5762 (ON CA)
Struck down the absolute prohibition on cannabis possession as applied to
a person with a serious medical condition lacking meaningful legal access.
Held the prohibition, without a constitutionally adequate medical
exemption, violated s.7. Parliament was given 12 months to fix the CDSA.

PILLAR 2 — R v Hitzig, 2003 CanLII 3611 (ON CA)
Held that the MMAR's supply provisions were unconstitutional because they
did not provide a practical, legal supply chain for authorized patients.
Core principle: a right of access without a practical way to exercise it
is not a right at all.

PILLAR 3 — R v Smith, 2015 SCC 34
The Supreme Court unanimously struck down the restriction limiting medical
cannabis authorization to dried plant form only, holding that patients
have a constitutional right to access non-dried forms (oils, edibles,
etc.) where a healthcare practitioner has authorized cannabis use. The
restriction was found arbitrary and overbroad relative to its purpose.

PILLAR 4 — Allard v Canada, 2016 FC 236
The Federal Court held that the MMPR's elimination of personal/designated
production violated s.7 by forcing patients into a commercial market many
could not afford, without a corresponding health/safety justification
proportionate to that burden.

PILLAR 5 — 2023 FC 1636 (ongoing s.7/s.15 challenges)
Continuing constitutional scrutiny of current ACMPR provisions; this
pillar is presented as evolving jurisprudence, not a settled holding —
treat any reliance on it as preliminary and verify its current status
before filing.

=== PART 3 — AFFIDAVIT STRUCTURE FOR A MEDICAL NECESSITY DEFENSE ===

AFFIDAVIT OF [YOUR FULL LEGAL NAME]
Sworn/Affirmed on [DATE]

I, [YOUR FULL LEGAL NAME], of the [CITY] of [CITY NAME], in the
[PROVINCE], MAKE OATH AND SAY (OR AFFIRM) AS FOLLOWS:

1. I am the Accused in this matter and have personal knowledge of the
   matters set out in this affidavit, except where stated to be based on
   information and belief, in which case I believe them to be true.

2. MEDICAL CONDITION: I have been diagnosed with [CONDITION] by
   [DOCTOR NAME / CLINIC], as set out in the medical records attached as
   Exhibit "A". [Describe how the condition affects daily life and why
   conventional treatments were inadequate, ineffective, or caused harmful
   side effects — be specific and factual.]

3. AUTHORIZATION/ATTEMPTS TO ACCESS LEGALLY: [Describe any medical
   document/authorization you held, OR describe specifically what
   barriers prevented you from obtaining one or from accessing a licensed
   producer — cost, distance, processing delays, denial, etc. Attach
   supporting documents as exhibits.]

4. WHY THE LEGAL FRAMEWORK FAILED ME: [Connect your specific facts to one
   or more of the five pillars above — e.g., "Like the patients in Allard
   v Canada, 2016 FC 236, the cost of purchasing from a licensed producer
   was prohibitive given my income of [AMOUNT], and personal production
   would have been the only realistic means of access."]

5. THE AMOUNT AND USE OF CANNABIS AT ISSUE: [State the quantity found/
   seized and explain how it corresponds to your documented medical need —
   courts scrutinize proportionality between the amount possessed and the
   claimed medical necessity closely.]

6. I make this affidavit in support of my application for [a stay of
   proceedings / a declaration of constitutional invalidity as applied to
   me / dismissal of the charge], and for no other or improper purpose.

=== PART 4 — NOTE ON SCOPE ===

A BENO-X defense is strongest where the accused can show a genuine,
documented medical need AND a specific, factual failure of the legal
access regime to meet that need — it is not a general legalization
argument and courts will treat it skeptically if not grounded in real
medical evidence. The current legal landscape (post-Cannabis Act 2018, for
non-medical use) is materially different from the medical-access era these
cases arose in; confirm with counsel whether and how this framework still
applies to your specific charge before relying on it.
