=== PART 1 — BACKGROUND: WHY DESIGNATED GROWERS EXIST ===

R v Hitzig, 2003 CanLII 3611 (ON CA) held that a medical access regime
without a practical legal supply chain is constitutionally inadequate — a
right of access without a way to actually obtain the substance is not a
meaningful right. The "designated grower" pathway (where an authorized
patient names another person to produce cannabis on their behalf) exists
because of this principle: not everyone can or wants to grow for
themselves, and personal production alone doesn't solve supply access for
patients who are unable to grow.

Sfetkopoulos v Canada (AG), 2008 FC 33 struck down an MMAR provision that
restricted a designated grower to producing for only one patient at a
time, as an unjustified barrier to the practical supply access Hitzig
required. [Verify current status/citation of this case with counsel —
confirm it remains good law and how it maps onto the ACMPR's current
provisions, which replaced the MMAR.]

=== PART 2 — STEP-BY-STEP REGISTRATION CHECKLIST ===

1. MEDICAL DOCUMENT — Obtain a medical document from an authorized
   healthcare practitioner (physician or nurse practitioner) specifying
   the daily authorized gram amount. This is the foundation of the entire
   application; without it, nothing below is possible.

2. CHOOSE YOUR ACCESS PATHWAY — Decide between:
   (a) Purchasing from a licensed producer/seller directly;
   (b) Registering to produce a limited amount for your own personal
       medical purposes; or
   (c) Designating another person to produce on your behalf.
   These are not mutually exclusive in all cases — confirm current Health
   Canada rules for your specific situation.

3. CALCULATE YOUR PLANT/PRODUCTION LIMITS — Production limits are
   calculated from your authorized daily gram amount using the applicable
   formula in effect at the time of your application (these formulas have
   changed over the life of the MMAR/ACMPR regimes — use the current
   Health Canada calculation tool, do not rely on an old formula).

4. IDENTIFY YOUR DESIGNATED GROWER (if applicable) — The designated
   grower must also complete a registration application, consent to the
   arrangement, and provide an address where production will occur (which
   may differ from the patient's address).

5. COMPLETE AND SUBMIT THE REGISTRATION APPLICATION — Through Health
   Canada's current portal/forms for the ACMPR program. Retain copies of
   everything submitted and all confirmation correspondence.

6. RETAIN PROOF OF EVERYTHING — Keep the medical document, the
   registration confirmation, any correspondence with Health Canada, and
   records of the production site address and plant count on hand at all
   times. In practice, this documentation is what protects a patient or
   designated grower during a police encounter — see the free "Arrest /
   Search Documentation Log" and "Know Your Rights Card" templates on this
   site for what to do if you're stopped despite valid registration.

=== PART 3 — COMMON PITFALLS ===

- Producing more plants than your current registration actually
  authorizes (e.g., after a doctor reduces your authorized daily amount,
  but you don't update your registration/production accordingly).
- Letting a registration lapse and continuing to produce without renewing
  it first.
- A designated-grower arrangement where the patient and grower disagree
  about authorized amounts or site address — keep this in writing and
  consistent with what's actually registered.
- Assuming a designated grower can produce for multiple patients without
  checking current Health Canada rules and ratios in effect at the time —
  these specific limits have changed historically (see Sfetkopoulos
  above) and may not be what they were when that case was decided.
- Treating an old printout of the regulations as current — confirm the
  rules in effect today before relying on anything in this package.
