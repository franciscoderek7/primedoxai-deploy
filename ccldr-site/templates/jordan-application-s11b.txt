NOTICE OF APPLICATION
(Charter of Rights and Freedoms, s.11(b) and s.24(1))

TAKE NOTICE that the Accused, [YOUR FULL LEGAL NAME], will make an
application to this Honourable Court on [DATE], at [TIME], or as soon
after as the application can be heard, at [COURTHOUSE NAME AND ADDRESS].

THE APPLICATION IS FOR:

1. A declaration that the Accused's right to be tried within a
   reasonable time, guaranteed by section 11(b) of the Canadian Charter
   of Rights and Freedoms, has been violated;

2. A stay of proceedings pursuant to section 24(1) of the Charter;

3. Such further and other relief as this Honourable Court deems just.

THE GROUNDS FOR THE APPLICATION ARE:

1. The Accused was charged on [DATE OF CHARGE];

2. As of the anticipated end of trial, the total elapsed time from the
   charge to the actual or anticipated end of trial will be
   approximately [X MONTHS];

3. Under R v Jordan, 2016 SCC 27, the presumptive ceiling for unreasonable
   delay is 18 months for matters tried in provincial court, and 30
   months for matters tried in superior court (or proceeding by
   preliminary inquiry). Delay exceeding the ceiling is presumptively
   unreasonable, and the burden shifts to the Crown to rebut the
   presumption with exceptional circumstances;

4. From the total delay, the following periods should be DEDUCTED as
   defence-caused delay or delay attributable to defence waiver (list
   only what genuinely applies — be exact and honest about this, since
   it is the section the Crown will scrutinize most closely):
   [LIST: e.g., adjournments requested by the defence, delay waived for
   defence counsel availability, etc. — or state "None" if none apply];

5. After deducting defence-caused delay, the net delay attributable to
   the Crown and the system is [X MONTHS], which exceeds the [18-month /
   30-month] presumptive ceiling;

6. [If under the ceiling: argue under the Morin framework as preserved in
   Jordan for delay below the ceiling — the defence must show the delay is
   markedly longer than reasonable and demonstrate meaningful prejudice.]

7. The Crown cannot establish exceptional circumstances (discrete events
   reasonably unforeseen/unavoidable, e.g. a complexity exception or a
   discrete and unforeseeable event) to justify the delay beyond the
   ceiling. [If the Crown intends to argue transitional exceptional
   circumstances for cases already in the system when Jordan was decided,
   address that directly.]

8. The Accused has suffered prejudice as a result of the delay, including:
   [DESCRIBE — e.g., ongoing bail conditions, employment/reputational
   harm, stress and anxiety, faded witness memory, lost evidence].

THIS APPLICATION will be supported by an affidavit of the Accused setting
out a detailed timeline of every appearance, adjournment, and the reason
for each (this timeline is the single most important document in a Jordan
application — courts expect a precise, date-by-date accounting), to be
served and filed prior to the hearing date.

⚖ EDUCATIONAL TEMPLATE ONLY — CCLDR.NET — NOT LEGAL ADVICE
This is a starting-point reference for understanding the structure of a
s.11(b)/Jordan delay application. It is not a complete or filing-ready
legal document, and a Jordan application is one of the most
fact-intensive, timeline-precise applications in criminal procedure —
errors in the delay calculation are common and can be fatal to the
application. Have this reviewed and adapted by a licensed lawyer before
filing. The presumptive ceilings and framework from R v Jordan, 2016 SCC 27
cited above should be independently verified by counsel against current
jurisprudence (including any subsequent SCC clarifications) before use in
any filing.
