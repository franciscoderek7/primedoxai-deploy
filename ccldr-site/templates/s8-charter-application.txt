                                                                       Accused

NOTICE OF APPLICATION
(Charter of Rights and Freedoms, s.8 and s.24(2))

TAKE NOTICE that the Accused, [YOUR FULL LEGAL NAME], will make an
application to this Honourable Court on [DATE], at [TIME], or as soon
after as the application can be heard, at [COURTHOUSE NAME AND ADDRESS].

THE APPLICATION IS FOR:

1. A declaration that the search of [the Accused / the Accused's vehicle /
   the Accused's residence] on [DATE OF SEARCH] violated section 8 of the
   Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms (the right to be secure against
   unreasonable search and seizure);

2. An order excluding [DESCRIBE THE EVIDENCE — e.g., "the cannabis seized
   from the vehicle's trunk"] from evidence pursuant to section 24(2) of
   the Charter;

3. Such further and other relief as this Honourable Court deems just.

THE GROUNDS FOR THE APPLICATION ARE:

1. On [DATE], the Accused was [stopped / searched / had property seized]
   by [OFFICER NAME(S) / BADGE NUMBER(S)] of the [POLICE SERVICE] at or
   near [LOCATION];

2. [DESCRIBE THE SEARCH — what was searched, with or without a warrant,
   with or without consent. If a warrant was relied upon, attach a copy of
   the Information to Obtain (ITO) and identify the specific defect being
   alleged — e.g., insufficient grounds, stale information, material
   non-disclosure to the issuing justice];

3. The search was conducted without a valid warrant and without lawful
   authority under a recognized exception to the warrant requirement
   (see Hunter v Southam Inc, [1984] 2 SCR 145 — a search is reasonable
   only if authorized by law, the law itself is reasonable, and the
   manner of the search is reasonable);

4. [IF WARRANTLESS: the search does not fall within a valid exception
   (e.g., search incident to arrest, plain view, exigent circumstances) —
   describe why. R v Collins, [1987] 1 SCR 265, sets out the framework for
   assessing reasonableness of a search.]

5. The Accused had a reasonable expectation of privacy in
   [the area/item searched] — see R v Tessling, 2004 SCC 67, on the
   factors relevant to assessing a reasonable expectation of privacy;

6. Pursuant to R v Grant, 2009 SCC 32, the Court must weigh: (a) the
   seriousness of the Charter-infringing state conduct; (b) the impact of
   the breach on the Charter-protected interests of the accused; and
   (c) society's interest in an adjudication on the merits. [Apply these
   three factors to your specific facts here.]

7. Admission of the evidence obtained as a result of this breach would
   bring the administration of justice into disrepute.

THIS APPLICATION will be supported by an affidavit of the Accused (and
any other supporting affidavits) to be served and filed prior to the
hearing date, and on such further evidence as counsel may advise.

DATED at [CITY], Ontario, this [DAY] day of [MONTH], [YEAR].
